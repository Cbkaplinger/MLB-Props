## 2. Corrected interpretation (owner round 4)

- **Headline (adopted wording):** "An opportunity-weighted,
  card-informed average rate improves the benchmark; modeling unequal
  PA probabilities adds no meaningful benefit under the tested
  assumptions."
- **I3b retains ordering information through p\*** = sum_t S(t) p_t /
  E[N]: which hitters are likely to receive more opportunities is
  still encoded. What I3b discards is only the unequal-probability
  COUNT-SHAPE increment after matching expected K. Ordering has not
  become irrelevant; the shape increment specifically failed to help.
- **Mechanism NOT established.** Call-ups / rest / lineup churn are
  plausible explanations for why the freshest observed nine carries
  information, but the experiment did not separately test them. It
  demonstrated useful opponent information - not why.
- **Source quality is NOT proven to be the binding constraint.** It is
  the next hypothesis. A better source could improve the forecast,
  leave it unchanged, or remove a predictive signal inadvertently
  supplied by the existing proxy (e.g., the first-nine-observed proxy
  may partly encode within-game usage/replacement patterns that a true
  starting card would not).
- **Dispersion mechanism remains unresolved.** Its persistence in
  Jul/Sep is consistent with BF-origin issues but does not prove
  BF errors caused it.
- **PIT mean 0.4929 alone is NOT a calibration finding** - a
  symmetric, badly misshapen PIT distribution can still average 0.5.
  The full 10-bin histogram (near-flat here: 501/471/461/443/411/406/
  390/448/434/481) is the descriptive evidence; shape deviations of
  that magnitude remain unadjudicated.
- **April tail sensitivity persists** - improving opponent
  information does not fix the inherited BF-tail problem; the 12+ lane
  (53 observed events) is NOT settled.
